\begin{abstract}
Generating plausible and coherent transitions between textured 3D objects remains challenging, largely due to the absence of ground-truth morphing trajectories. Recent approaches leverage pretrained 3D generative models to synthesize intermediate states, but rely on predefined interpolation strategies rather than learning how objects transform across diverse examples.
In this work, we introduce \methodname{}, a framework that turns generated morphing trajectories into supervision for learning a generalizable 3D morphing function. By training across diverse source objects, our model learns to combine their geometric and appearance information directly from 3D representations, replacing fixed interpolation rules with adaptive source conditioning. To mitigate artifacts inherited from generated supervision, we further incorporate a pretrained generative prior that guides the learning process toward plausible intermediate states. Experiments demonstrate that \methodname{} produces more regular perceptual transitions and improves visual quality compared to recent training-free approaches, while achieving faster generation than state-of-the-art methods. Code is available in the supplementary materials.
\end{abstract}
